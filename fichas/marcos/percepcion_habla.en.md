The **categorical perception** tasks (Serniclaes battery) use synthetic syllables ranging from BA to PA in voicing steps. In identification, the child says which syllable they hear; in discrimination, whether two syllables are the same or different. **Minimal pairs** ask the child to decide whether two real syllables are the same or different. They measure the precision of phonemic categories, the basis of letter–sound correspondences. **Phonological fluency** (saying, in 30 seconds, words that begin with a given phoneme) measures something else: lexical access from a sound and executive control.

**What the evidence says:**

- **Dyslexia shows a categorical perception deficit, clearer in discrimination.** A meta-analysis confirms the deficit compared with children of the same age and with younger readers at the same level. The effect is larger in discrimination than in identification [@noordenbos2015meta].
- **In Spanish, what fails is the boundary between categories.** In 204 Spanish children, those with dyslexia discriminated pairs that cross the phonemic boundary worse and some within-category differences better (allophonic perception) [@serniclaes2021allophonic]. That is why the total of correct responses mixes two effects of opposite sign.
- **Its predictive value is modest and not entirely phonological.** In 237 children, perception at age 5½ predicted reading a year later without going through phonemic awareness. These tasks also capture decision making [@snowling2019speech]. In another study, its effect on decoding disappeared when phonological processing was controlled [@mcbridechang1997growth]. The evidence is therefore contradictory.
- **Phonological fluency reflects access, not representations.** In 66 Greek children with dyslexia or developmental language disorder, word production was lower, but the size of clusters of related words was the same as in 83 controls [@mengisidou2019fluency].

**What our data show.**

- **Categorical perception.** Discrimination does not predict persistent difficulty (AUC .39–.53) and identification barely does (.51–.63). This is to be expected if the total of correct responses is scored: the identification slope and between- and within-category discrimination should be computed from the item-by-item responses.
- **Minimal pairs.** Low stability (ρ = .07–.39) and AUC from .45 to .69, better from age 5. Against Woodcock-Muñoz, .63–.66 at ages 6–7.
- **Phonological fluency.** A single session at age 5 (793 children, almost all from Preleo schools): AUC of .68 against persistent difficulty and of .59–.62 against low comprehension. It is related to phonological awareness, letters and reading (ρ = .41–.43).
