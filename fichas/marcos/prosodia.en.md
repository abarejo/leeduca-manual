The stress tasks measure **sensitivity to lexical stress**: perceiving which syllable sounds stronger. In Spanish, stress distinguishes words (*término*, *termino*, *terminó*), so it is part of the phonological representation of each word. In **word stress** (ages 6–7, 18 items), the child hears a three-syllable word and points to the stressed syllable. In **pseudoword stress** (ages 4–5), the child hears two almost identical sequences and decides whether they are the same or different. The hypothesis is that sensitivity to speech rhythm helps segment words and, through that route, learn to read.

**What the evidence says:**

- **In Spanish, suprasegmental sensitivity predicts reading beyond phonological awareness.** In 92 Grade 5 children, it explained unique variance in reading once phonological awareness and vocabulary were controlled [@calet2016suprasegmental].
- **In preschool, its effect goes through phonological awareness.** In 132 Spanish schoolchildren, preschoolers' lexical prosody influenced word and pseudoword reading only through phonological awareness [@roldan2026prosodic]. In 370 English-speaking Grade 1 children, the relation of stress with word reading was fully mediated by phonological and morphological awareness [@kim2016prosodic]. In 45 preschool children, lexical stress did not explain unique variance in reading once phonological awareness was controlled [@goodman2010stress].

Overall, the relation with reading is consistent, but its independent contribution is doubtful at the early ages, which are precisely those of these tasks.

**What our data show.**

- **Word stress.** α (2021) = .74, with hardly any ceiling effect (4%). Against persistent difficulty, AUC of .64–.66; against later low comprehension, only .53–.55, and against Woodcock-Muñoz, .52–.63. It is weakly related to reading and comprehension (ρ = .15–.26) and not at all to RAN.
- **Pseudoword stress.** Stability ρ = .33 between two terms at age 4. Validity decreases with age: AUC of .68 at 4.1, .62 at 4.2 and .55 at age 5. It is related to phonological awareness (ρ ≈ .30) and not to RAN, as expected of a phonological skill.
- **Caution.** The maximum score goes from 13 at 4.1 to 31 in the following sessions: these are probably different versions of the task and should not be compared without reviewing them.
