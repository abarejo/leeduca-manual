Rapid naming of pictures measures how quickly the child retrieves and says, in series, very familiar names; it is one of the best predictors of reading fluency. The variants change:

- the **phonological similarity** of the names: same initial syllable, same initial phoneme, or unrelated names (control);
- the **length** of the names: short or long;
- **repetition**: five repeated pictures or varied pictures, on a card or one at a time.

**What the evidence says:**

- **RAN predicts reading, also with pictures and before reading.** A meta-analysis of 137 studies (28,826 participants) finds a moderate to strong relation (r = .43). Stimulus type is one of the most influential moderators [@araujo2014ran]. In a three-year longitudinal study, non-alphanumeric RAN measured before reading instruction predicted fluency growth, with no reverse effect of reading on RAN [@lervag2009ran].
- **In Spanish, pictures and alphanumeric stimuli form distinct factors.** In 874 Spanish children from Grade 2 to Grade 6, RAN tasks group into an alphanumeric and a non-alphanumeric factor, stable across grades [@rodriguez2015ran].
- **Phonological similarity adds a load that affects dyslexia more, but with letters.** Readers with dyslexia are more impaired than others when adjacent letters sound similar [@jones2013ran]. We have not found studies that manipulate picture names in this way: this variant is specific to the platform and its interpretation is, for now, a hypothesis.
- **Format matters.** In Grade 1, serial RAN is more strongly related to reading than discrete RAN (one stimulus at a time) [@dejong2011discrete].

**What our data show.** These are class B and C tasks, with few sessions per variant:

- **Stability.** ρ = .33 (control), .42 (same syllable) and .41 (same phoneme) across terms from age 4 to 6; .45 for the long versions at ages 7 to 8.
- **Validity.** Against persistent difficulty, AUC from .55 to .72 at ages 4 to 6 and from .57 to .74 at ages 7–8. Against later low comprehension, only .52 to .63. Against Woodcock-Muñoz, maximum AUC of .70.
- **Phonological similarity.** The same-phoneme version reaches .72 at age 6, compared with .65–.67 for the control, but the intervals overlap: there is no clear advantage in manipulating phonology.
- **Caution.** Mean times change a lot between sessions of the same school year (in the control version, 25.5 s at 4.1 and 44.5 s at 4.2). Norms must be computed by session and the administered version reviewed.
