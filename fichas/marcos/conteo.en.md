Counting the kittens on a picture card measures **counting of collections**: assigning a numeral to each object and knowing that the last one says how many there are (cardinality). Counting forwards within the first ten measures only the verbal sequence, without objects. Both are treated in more detail in the number knowledge framework.

**What the evidence says:**

- **Cardinality is one of the best early predictors.** In 197 children assessed at the start of preschool, knowledge of the cardinal value of numerals, fluency in the number sequence and digit recognition predicted mathematics at the end of the stage [@geary2016core]. In 128 British children, cardinality and receptive vocabulary explained the initial differences in mathematics [@cahoon2024cognitive].
- **In Spanish, the verbal sequence carries most weight.** In children followed from preschool to Grade 2, procedural counting was the task with the most weight in later mathematics achievement [@mercader2017basicas].

**What our data show.** Both tasks have a strong ceiling at age 5: 41–47% of children get all counting items right and 86% the whole sequence. That is why they discriminate poorly: AUC .43–.60 against low arithmetic at ages 10–11 and .45–.58 against low reading fluency. Consistency is moderate (α = .68 and .74) and the stability of counting is low (ρ = .19). Their reasonable use is to detect the few children who have not yet mastered counting in preschool.
