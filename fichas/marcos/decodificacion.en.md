Reading syllables, words and pseudowords aloud measures **written word recognition**: converting letters into sounds and accessing the word. It is the decoding component of the simple view of reading [@hoover1990simple] and the core of dyslexia according to the IDA definition (@sec-definicion).

**What distinguishes each list.** Dual-route models separate two procedures. The **sublexical route** converts graphemes into phonemes and is the only one that works for pseudowords. The **lexical route** recognizes the whole word, and it shows in words being read faster than pseudowords (lexicality effect) and frequent words faster than rare ones. In Spanish, children use both from Grade 2 [@avdyli2014lexical; @davies2013lexical]. That is why the platform has both lists: pseudowords measure pure decoding, and the difference with words estimates how much orthographic lexicon the child has built. Length (from one to four syllables) makes serial reading visible: the more the child depends on letter-by-letter conversion, the more each added syllable costs.

**In a transparent orthography, what discriminates is speed.** In Spanish, almost all children read accurately by the middle of Grade 1. Children with dyslexia are accurate but read more slowly, and their reading resembles that of younger children of the same reading level: it is delayed rather than deviant development [@davies2007spanish]. A meta-analysis of 79 European studies concludes that reading speed distinguishes readers with dyslexia in all orthographies and ages, whereas accuracy depends on age and transparency [@carioti2021depth]. In German, 70% of slow readers in Grade 1 were still slow in Year 8 [@landerl2008fluency]. That is why the score in these task sheets is **correct responses per minute**, not correct responses.

**What our data show.** These are the most reliable and valid tasks on the platform:

- **Reliability.** Stability between terms of ρ = .65 to .81, and 2021 α between .73 and .97.
- **External validity.** They predict low Woodcock-Muñoz reading fluency years later with a median AUC of .72 to .77 ([chapter](../../criterio-woodcock.en.qmd)). The longer lists (three- and four-syllable words) predict best.
- **Delay or deviance.** Comparing pseudowords with words among children of the same reading level separates delay from deviance ([Norms by reading level](../../baremo-nivel-lector.en.qmd)).
