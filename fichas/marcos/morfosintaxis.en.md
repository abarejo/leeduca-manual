These tasks measure **morphological and syntactic knowledge of oral language**: inflecting words and pseudowords for gender, number and tense, ordering words to form a sentence, and choosing the picture that matches a sentence. With pseudowords, the child has to apply the rule because they cannot rely on a known word. In the simple view of reading they are part of language comprehension [@hoover1990simple].

**What the evidence says:**

- **Morphology is related to reading, also in the long term.** A meta-analysis of 44 studies finds a correlation of .57 between morphological awareness and reading comprehension [@liu2023morphological]. Another, with 35 longitudinal studies, estimates .48 with later word reading and .55 with comprehension, more with accuracy than with fluency [@bodynek2026longitudinal].
- **In Spanish, morphology helps comprehension, not decoding.** In Grade 4 its effect on comprehension is only direct and does not go through word reading, because grapheme–phoneme rules are enough for pronunciation [@dalessio2019morphological]. In 234 Grade 4 children from Seville, syntax predicted comprehension after controlling for decoding and vocabulary; morphology added nothing [@simpson2020morphological].
- **Syntax has a role of its own.** Syntactic awareness in Grade 3 predicts progress in reading comprehension up to Grade 4, but not word reading [@deacon2018syntactic]. Language at age 3½, grammar included, predicts comprehension at age 8½ [@hulme2015foundations].

Almost all the evidence comes from children aged 8 to 10. There is little in Spanish with oral tasks at ages 4–6, the ages of our tasks.

**What our data show.** These are class B and C tasks, with few items.

- **Low consistency and stability.** α = .57–.71. Stability across terms is very low: ρ = .22 for gender and .05 for number with words.
- **Ceiling and floor.** 48% of 6-year-olds get everything right in gender with pseudowords; 25% get nothing right in number with pseudowords.
- **What predicts best.** Grammatical reception and sentence ordering: AUC .63–.69 against the reading difficulty label and .57–.66 against later low comprehension. Morphology with words stays close to chance (AUC .45–.62).

They are best used as complementary information on oral language, not for individual screening.
