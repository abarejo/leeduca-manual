Phonological awareness is the ability to reflect on and manipulate the sounds of speech. The platform's tasks cover a continuum of difficulty:

- by **unit**: syllable or phoneme;
- by **operation**: counting, identifying, segmenting or deleting;
- by **position**: initial, final or any.

The "all positions" version mixes items and is the most demanding.

**What the evidence says:**

- **Phonemes and manipulation are what most distinguish dyslexia.** In 2,252 children from six European languages, phoneme deletion and RAN were the strongest concurrent predictors of dyslexia [@landerl2013predictors]. In consistent orthographies, children with dyslexia perform below even younger readers of the same reading level on phoneme manipulation tasks, but not on syllable tasks [@parrila2020rlmatch].
- **In Spanish, phonological awareness develops in stages**, from the syllable to the phoneme. Phonemic awareness builds on learning to read and at the same time drives it: the relation is reciprocal [@denton2000pa]. Phonological awareness predicts above all the reading of complex syllables [@martinez2020silabica].
- **Its predictive value is universal, but changes with age.** Phonemic awareness, letters and RAN predict reading growth equally in Spanish, Czech and English [@caravolas2013growth]. Its relation with fluency, however, is more complex and depends on task and age, whereas RAN predicts fluency in all languages [@landerl2019pa]. This is what @catts2026definicion lead us to expect: precursors are useful for early prognosis and lose weight once there is reading to measure.

The pilot task sheets develop this framework in more detail: [syllable counting](../CF523.en.qmd) and [rapid naming](../RAN.en.qmd).

**What our data show.** The pattern matches the literature:

- **What predicts best.** Deletion tasks in all positions are those that best predict low Woodcock-Muñoz fluency (median AUC .72–.73, up to .81).
- **In between.** The other deletion and identification tasks (.63–.68).
- **What predicts least.** Phoneme segmentation and syllable counting (.60–.62).
- **Consistency and stability.** Internal consistency is good (α = .82–.94), but stability between terms is low (ρ = .27–.41). Many tasks were given in only one or two sessions, and children change quickly at these ages. For an individual decision, the result should be confirmed at two time points.
