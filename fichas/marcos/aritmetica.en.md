These tasks measure **arithmetic fluency**: solving simple additions and subtractions quickly, above all by retrieving arithmetic facts from memory. There are two formats. In the **production** formats (ages 6–8), the child says the result aloud and the examiner records it. In the **verification** formats (codes 107xx, school year 2021/22), the child sees the operation with a result and decides whether it is correct; they are self-administered and time-limited (60 or 120 s).

**What the evidence says:**

- **Producing and verifying are not exactly the same.** Production and verification errors are primed separately: verification relies more on the familiarity of the presented result [@campbell1996retrieval]. Scores from the two formats are not interchangeable.
- **Arithmetic and reading fluency share predictors.** In 200 Finnish children followed from age 5 to 10, counting and RAN predicted both calculation and reading fluency [@koponen2013counting]. A common factor of RAN and verbal counting was the best predictor of what the two fluencies share [@koponen2020serial].
- **Phonological awareness is associated with fact retrieval, not with procedural calculation.** In 63 children with learning difficulties, phonemic awareness was related to problems solved from memory and not to those that require counting [@matejko2022phonological].
- **Difficulties are persistent and can be anticipated in preschool.** In 2,518 Finnish children followed from Grade 1 to Grade 9, 12% had persistent arithmetic fluency difficulties; preschool counting, number concepts and RAN distinguished this profile [@psyridou2023profiles].

**What our data show.**

- **Production.** One-digit additions and two-digit additions and subtractions have acceptable consistency (α = .72–.76). They predict low arithmetic at ages 10–11 (AUC .59–.71) and, to the same extent, low Woodcock-Muñoz fluency (AUC .67–.74). The two-digit version has a ceiling at age 7 (23–33% maximum scores) and its screen shows a result, although the instructions ask the child to solve: which format was administered needs to be confirmed.
- **Verification.** We do not yet have α for these versions. Their relation with concurrent low reading fluency is moderate (AUC .58–.69). The task for ages 10–11 (code 10748) is itself the low-arithmetic criterion ([Numerical cognition](../../cognicion-numerica.en.qmd#sec-mate-criterio)) and cannot be validated against it.
- **Comorbidity.** Of 799 children aged 10–11, 19 have both low arithmetic and low reading, compared with 12.3 expected by chance. Together with the above, this supports a shared retrieval fluency.
