**Visual-attentional span** is the number of visual elements processed simultaneously within a string. On the platform, a string of letters, numbers or symbols is briefly presented and the child chooses what they saw. **Serial search** asks whether there is an O among groups of Qs of different sizes. The **sustained attention** task (UNED battery) consists of 50 trials in which the child decides whether the name they hear matches the picture; despite its name, the catalog describes it as an index of inhibitory control. Reading requires processing several letters at once and maintaining attention along the line.

**What the evidence says:**

- **Visual-attentional span predicts reading beyond phonemic awareness.** In 417 French children in Grades 1, 3 and 5, it predicted reading independently of phonemic awareness, especially for irregular words [@bosse2009span]. Orthographic depth modulates how this attention is distributed: advanced bilinguals reading in French and Basque distributed it more evenly than those reading in Spanish and Basque [@antzaka2018depth].
- **Visual search is associated with reading, with small samples.** In 16 children with dyslexia and 16 controls, slow serial search was associated with visual-attentional span and with irregular word reading, not with phonology [@lallier2013search]. Visual scanning measured in preschool predicted learning to read in Italian children [@ferretti2008scanning].
- **Sustained attention is related more to comprehension than to word reading.** In 245 poor readers in Grades 4 and 5, it explained unique variance in comprehension, but not in reading accuracy or fluency [@macdonald2021sustained].

The evidence is weaker than for phonological processes and there are almost no studies in Spanish.

**What our data show.**

- **Letter and symbol subtests.** With 6–10 points and a single session, AUC from .47 to .61: separately they are not interpretable.
- **Numbers and letters (36 items, ages 4–5).** α = .60 and AUC of .66–.67 against persistent difficulty and of .65 against later low comprehension. It is the best in the family, as expected of alphanumeric stimuli.
- **Numbers (16 items).** α = .56 and almost zero stability across school years (ρ = .05–.19). AUC from .54 to .69.
- **Serial search.** α = .74, ceiling of 11–20% and AUC from .58 to .63.
- **Sustained attention.** Despite an α of .95, it shows no validity: extreme ceiling (median 45 out of 50), stability of ρ = .03–.16, null correlation with the other domains and AUC from .43 to .56. It should not be used until the data have been reviewed.
