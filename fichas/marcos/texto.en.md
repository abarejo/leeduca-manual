Reading a continuous text measures **reading fluency**: reading accurately, at a good pace and with the intonation that the meaning requires. It integrates word recognition with sentence comprehension processes, which is why it is the bridge between decoding and comprehension in the simple view of reading [@hoover1990simple]. The platform measures two readings of the same story: the first, for speed, and a second aimed at comprehension.

**What the evidence says:**

- **Speed and comprehension go together in Spanish.** In a transparent orthography, decoding is mastered early and its weight in comprehension decreases across school years, while that of oral language increases [@sanchezvincitore2022svr; @lervag2018comprehension]. Text reading speed is the decoding measure that remains related to comprehension the longest.
- **It is the most stable measure of reading skill** in consistent orthographies. In German, slowness in Grade 1 anticipated slowness years later [@landerl2008fluency].
- **There are references in Spanish.** The meta-analysis by @ripoll2020velocidad summarizes speeds by grade in Spanish speakers. Grade explains around 70% of the differences between studies, and speed changes with the term in the first school years.

**What our data show.** Stability between terms is ρ = .63 to .65. The 2021 α for text speed is low (.40), but α is not a good indicator for a timed task. It predicts low Woodcock-Muñoz fluency with a median AUC of .74 to .75, and up to .84–.87 at the best time points.
