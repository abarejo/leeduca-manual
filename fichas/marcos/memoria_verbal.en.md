Repeating sequences of nonsense syllables, from one to five syllables, measures **phonological short-term memory**: keeping in order a series of sounds that are not supported by meaning. It is related to reading because decoding requires holding the sounds of the letters while they are blended. The versions use CV or CVC syllables.

**What the evidence says:**

- **It adds little beyond phonemic awareness.** A meta-analysis concludes that phonemic awareness is the strongest unique predictor of reading. Longitudinal and experimental studies support its causal role, but not that of verbal short-term memory [@melbylervag2012stm]. In a study from preschool to Grade 3, verbal memory did not predict reading once phonological awareness and RAN were controlled [@parrila2004predictors].
- **Serial order does seem to count at the start.** In 780 children followed from age 4 to 9, serial order memory predicted word reading from age 4 to 6, and reading did not influence it. Pseudoword repetition and reading influenced each other in both directions [@cunningham2021memory].
- **In dyslexia the deficit exists, but it depends on oral language.** Children with dyslexia repeat pseudowords worse than children of their age and than younger readers at the same reading level. Severity varies widely across studies and is explained mainly by oral language level, that is, by co-occurrence with developmental language disorder [@melbylervag2012nonword].

We have not found longitudinal studies in Spanish with this task.

**What our data show.**

- **Main version (15 items, ages 4 to 7).** α (2021) = .76 and stability across school years ρ = .36 (range .23–.44, about 12 months apart). Its validity grows with age: AUC against persistent difficulty of .58 at age 4, .61 at age 5 and .66–.72 at ages 6–7. Against later low comprehension, .58–.65. The ceiling effect is moderate (4–12%).
- **Full version at age 8.** AUC of .63 against persistent difficulty and of .62–.66 against Woodcock-Muñoz.
- **CVC version of 1–5 syllables (age 7).** The observed maximum is 3, 86% of children are at ceiling and the AUC is .50. This looks like a recording or scoring error, not a result about the construct: it should not be used until it has been reviewed.
