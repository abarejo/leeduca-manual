These tasks measure **receptive oral language**. In listening comprehension, the child hears a text without seeing it and answers questions by choosing among options. In multiple-choice vocabulary, the child hears a word and chooses among four pictures. In the simple view of reading, listening comprehension is the component that does not depend on reading [@hoover1990simple]. The vocabulary framework covers expressive and reading vocabulary.

**What the evidence says:**

- **In transparent orthographies, listening comprehension is the best predictor of reading comprehension.** In 1,895 Italian children from Grade 1 to Grade 5, listening comprehension predicted reading comprehension in every grade and reading accuracy played a smaller role [@tobia2015simple]. In 1,815 Finnish children, the effect of fluency disappeared after Grade 1 and that of listening comprehension remained until Grade 3 [@torppa2016simple]. In 4,750 Dominican schoolchildren, its weight grows as reading comprehension improves [@sanchezvincitore2022svr].
- **Preschool oral language anticipates reading comprehension.** In children aged 4 to 6, inferences, literal comprehension of a story and grammar predicted reading comprehension a year later; vocabulary acted through them [@silva2014comprehension]. Language in a broad sense predicts more than vocabulary alone [@lervag2018comprehension].
- **Receptive vocabulary predicts level, not rate.** In two Australian samples (N = 4,983 and 4,570), receptive vocabulary at age 4 predicted comprehension at age 8, but its growth was not related to reading growth between ages 8 and 14 [@larsen2026receptive].

The relation is weak in the first grades, when decoding limits comprehension, and grows later. These tasks can be expected to predict little in the short term.

**What our data show.** They are the weakest tasks in their domain:

- **Low consistency.** Listening comprehension has α = .30 with 10 items; multiple-choice vocabulary, α = .51. The latter is also very unstable across terms (ρ = .12–.14) and its observed maximum (25) exceeds the number of items (17), which needs to be reviewed.
- **Low predictive validity.** Against low reading comprehension at ages 7 and 8, the AUC is .58–.59 for listening comprehension and .52–.58 for vocabulary.

They are not suitable for individual decisions. Before using them in articles, the items and scoring should be reviewed.
