Normative study

Status: first draft (November 2, 2026) of chapter 4 of the manual, computed with cap4_estudio_normativo_v1.R on the long table v2 (2010–2015 cohorts). It uses only aggregated tables, with cells of fewer than 10 children suppressed and schools pseudonymized. There are three open issues to resolve before the sample is considered final; they are explained at the end. Translated from the Spanish version of November 27, 2026.

In one sentence. The normative sample brings together 10,278 child-by-cohort records from six cohorts (born 2010 to 2015), assessed in schools in the province of Málaga from age 4 or 5. Up to the 2019/20 school year children were followed three times per year; in 2020/21 and 2021/22 there was a single, much broader assessment per year.

Sample flow diagram

The diagram summarizes the following sections. Missing records are described in 4.5; follow-up by age in 4.3; the Preleo condition in 4.4. The reference sample for the norms is that of the non-Preleo schools (see Methodological decisions). In the 2011 and 2015 cohorts all schools in the follow-up implemented Preleo, so they contribute no reference children.

Caution. The condition of 299 children from 2013 recorded as Preleo and 68 from 2014 recorded as non-Preleo is under review (see 4.4). Until then, the diagram counts them as recorded in assignment v6.

4.1 Cohorts and schools

Each cohort was assessed in between 31 and 72 schools. The 2010 to 2013 cohorts are the large ones (about 2,000 children each); the 2014 and 2015 cohorts, with fewer than 800, only reach preschool and Grade 1. The proportion of girls is between 46% and 51%, and sex is recorded for 93% of the children.

Mean age at first assessment varies a lot between cohorts because not all children enter at the same time: many appear for the first time in the broad assessments of 2020/21 and 2021/22 (see 4.2). To describe age, it is better to report it at a fixed time point (for example, 6.1) rather than at first assessment.

4.2 Children by time point

The map shows two different designs within the same database:

  • Term-by-term follow-up (to 2019/20). Three assessments per school year from age 4 (2011–2015 cohorts) or age 5 (2010 cohort), with between 300 and 950 children per time point and gradual attrition with age.
  • Broad assessments of 2020/21 and 2021/22 (orange border). A single assessment per year, in February, with many new children. After the pandemic began, paper versions were administered, mainly of efficiency tests, from Grade 2 to Grade 6 (tabulated data in 03_PAPEL_TABLETS_PANDEMIA; the TECLE, PEALE and syntax booklets are in the folder of the Colombia project). They are two different batteries (Table 3.3):
    • February 2021, with about 1,300–1,560 children per cohort: the full TECLE battery (TECLE, semantic relations, syntax and reading vocabulary).
    • School year 2021/22, with about 260–470 children per cohort: text comprehension, Woodcock, lexical decision, homographs, reading comprehension, reading speed and mathematics (and, in the 2014 and 2015 cohorts, phoneme segmentation and initial letter).

Paper or platform? The structure of the output settles this (cap4_estructura_sesiones_v1.R). The 2021 assessment (session 2021) only contains the number of responses, correct responses and errors per test, with no times: it is the tabulation of the paper booklets. The 2021/22 assessment (sessions 948–950) has the signature of the new platform, with correct responses, errors, total time and efficiency per task and instance codes, and was administered on tablets (the export “SESION 948 TABLETS” is in 03_PAPEL_TABLETS_PANDEMIA). Its tasks have a time limit: word and pseudoword lists stop at 45 s and Woodcock and homographs at 180 s, so the score is how many items are read in that time, not how long it takes to read the list.

This has a practical consequence: time point 8.2 of the 2012 cohort is the 2020/21 assessment, and the same applies to 7.2 of 2013, 9.2 of 2011 and 10.2 of 2010. The drop in accuracy at 8.2 that had been treated as a data issue is probably explained by this change of format (paper) and of sample, not by a change of list. The two assessments are treated as a separate cross-sectional design, each with its own format: they are not mixed with the term-by-term follow-up in longitudinal analyses and, if norms are built for them, they are built separately.

4.3 Follow-up and attrition

The median number of time points per child is low (1 or 2) because the broad assessments add many children with a single assessment. Half of the children or more appear only in the broad assessments: 1,220 of 2,386 in the 2010 cohort, 1,094 of 2,321 in 2011 and 1,469 of 1,937 in 2012. The longitudinal core is therefore much smaller: in the 2010 cohort, 536 children have data at age 5 and at 8 or older, and in 2011, 409; counting only the term-by-term follow-up assessments (without the broad ones), 481 and 379 remain. Longitudinal analyses work with that core, and each one must check whether those who stay resemble those who leave: in the longitudinal analyses, reading at age 6 does not differ between those who reach age 8 and those who leave earlier.

4.4 Preleo condition and socioeconomic status

With the condition restricted to the term-by-term follow-up, the table matches what the team describes: in 2010, 843 children from 23 schools with the program and 320 from 12 without it (the thesis counts 19 and 11 schools); in 2011, the whole follow-up cohort with Preleo (1,227 children); 2012, without the program. About 1,000–1,500 children per cohort have an unknown condition because they appear only in the 2020/21 and 2021/22 assessments.

The cleanest contrast is still that between the 2013 (no program) and 2014 (program) cohorts in the same schools. In the data, however, 299 children from 2013 (14 schools) are recorded as Preleo and 68 from 2014 as non-Preleo, which needs to be checked with A. Sánchez.

The mean socioeconomic index of the schools ranges from 4.0 to 5.5 depending on cohort and condition, with an SD of about 2.4 points. In 2010, the 5 non-Preleo schools with a known index have a somewhat higher index than the 18 Preleo schools (5.4 vs. 4.7), which must be controlled for when estimating effects. [To be completed: scale and source of the SES index.]

4.5 Cleaning and missing data

Non-numeric values (between 0.4% and 11.9% depending on the cohort) are mostly empty cells for tasks not administered. Times recorded as 0 are treated as missing. In the 2010 cohort there are 20,025 records with no age assigned, which need to be checked in the session dictionary.

Decisions and pending issues

  1. Preleo condition (decided November 2, 2026). The preferred source is the information from Auxiliadora Sánchez: in the 2010 cohort, the classification in her thesis (19 schools with Preleo and 11 without it, with implementation fidelity); in the 2011 cohort, all children are Preleo. Implemented in assignment v5. Also decided (November 2, 2026): the condition applies only to children in the term-by-term follow-up; for those who appear only in the broad assessments it stays “unknown”, because their schools are not in the Preleo follow-up for their cohort (assignment v6, asignar_preleo_v6.R; the table in 4.4 already uses it). The 2012 cohort and the 2013 and 2014 exceptions also need to be checked with A. Sánchez.
  2. School names (done). Version v5 uses the v2 matching (by similarity and with manual checking) as the canonical school: 65–72 schools in the large cohorts and 31 in the small ones, compared with 79–96 names on the platform. Between 209 and 346 children per cohort remain without a matched school, mostly from schools that appear only in the broad assessments.
  3. 2020/21 and 2021/22 assessments (done). 2021: TECLE battery on paper, with no times. 2021/22: platform on tablets, with a time limit. It remains to decide whether separate norms will be built and how they are cited in articles that use reading at age 8 or older.
