Theoretical framework

Reading a continuous text measures reading fluency: reading accurately, at a good pace and with the intonation that the meaning requires. It integrates word recognition with sentence comprehension processes, which is why it is the bridge between decoding and comprehension in the simple view of reading (Hoover and Gough 1990). The platform measures two readings of the same story: the first, for speed, and a second aimed at comprehension.

What the evidence says:

  • Speed and comprehension go together in Spanish. In a transparent orthography, decoding is mastered early and its weight in comprehension decreases across school years, while that of oral language increases (Sánchez-Vincitore et al. 2022; Lervåg, Hulme, and Melby-Lervåg 2018). Text reading speed is the decoding measure that remains related to comprehension the longest.
  • It is the most stable measure of reading skill in consistent orthographies. In German, slowness in Grade 1 anticipated slowness years later (Landerl and Wimmer 2008).
  • There are references in Spanish. The meta-analysis by Ripoll Salceda, Tapia Montesinos, and Aguado Alonso (2020) summarizes speeds by grade in Spanish speakers. Grade explains around 70% of the differences between studies, and speed changes with the term in the first school years.

What our data show. Stability between terms is ρ = .63 to .65. The 2021 α for text speed is low (.40), but α is not a good indicator for a timed task. It predicts low Woodcock-Muñoz fluency with a median AUC of .74 to .75, and up to .84–.87 at the best time points. The comparison with the literature and the norms in words per minute are in the reading speed task sheet.

Forms: can they be pooled?

Forms given at the same time point are compared. They are considered equivalent if the mean difference is smaller than 0.20 SD and the SD ratio is between 0.80 and 1.25. If the same children took them, linear equating is also computed, which puts the second form on the scale of the first, together with the correlation between forms (parallel-forms reliability). If the groups differ, the difference mixes form and cohort.

For publications

The LEEDUCA platform task “Text reading rate” (codes 100, 10737, 10740, 1090, 2034, 2035, 3979 and 4099; EDICOLE and LEEDUCA battery) assesses reading fluency. The child reads aloud the stimuli shown on the screen and the examiner records each correct response or error. It comprises 60 items. The time limit is 30 seconds. It is administered individually on a computer at school. In the 2010, 2011, 2012, 2013, 2014 and 2015 cohorts it was administered in 28 sessions, from 5 to 11 years of age. The main outcome is the efficiency (correct responses per unit of time). In our data (2021 analyses), internal consistency was α = .40 (N = 859, 60 items). Stability across successive administrations (median interval of 4 months) was ρ = .75 (median of 18 session pairs; range -0.54–.86). Because the task is timed, α tends to overestimate reliability; stability across administrations is the preferred estimate. Stability across time points was ρ = -.02–.86. Against later poor reading comprehension, AUC = .48–.65.
