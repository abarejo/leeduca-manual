Theoretical framework

The stress tasks measure sensitivity to lexical stress: perceiving which syllable sounds stronger. In Spanish, stress distinguishes words (término, termino, terminó), so it is part of the phonological representation of each word. In word stress (ages 6–7, 18 items), the child hears a three-syllable word and points to the stressed syllable. In pseudoword stress (ages 4–5), the child hears two almost identical sequences and decides whether they are the same or different. The hypothesis is that sensitivity to speech rhythm helps segment words and, through that route, learn to read.

What the evidence says:

  • In Spanish, suprasegmental sensitivity predicts reading beyond phonological awareness. In 92 Grade 5 children, it explained unique variance in reading once phonological awareness and vocabulary were controlled (Calet et al. 2016).
  • In preschool, its effect goes through phonological awareness. In 132 Spanish schoolchildren, preschoolers’ lexical prosody influenced word and pseudoword reading only through phonological awareness (Roldán et al. 2026). In 370 English-speaking Grade 1 children, the relation of stress with word reading was fully mediated by phonological and morphological awareness (Kim and Petscher 2016). In 45 preschool children, lexical stress did not explain unique variance in reading once phonological awareness was controlled (Goodman, Libenson, and Wade-Woolley 2010).

Overall, the relation with reading is consistent, but its independent contribution is doubtful at the early ages, which are precisely those of these tasks.

What our data show.

  • Word stress. α (2021) = .74, with hardly any ceiling effect (4%). Against persistent difficulty, AUC of .64–.66; against later low comprehension, only .53–.55, and against Woodcock-Muñoz, .52–.63. It is weakly related to reading and comprehension (ρ = .15–.26) and not at all to RAN.
  • Pseudoword stress. Stability ρ = .33 between two terms at age 4. Validity decreases with age: AUC of .68 at 4.1, .62 at 4.2 and .55 at age 5. It is related to phonological awareness (ρ ≈ .30) and not to RAN, as expected of a phonological skill.
  • Caution. The maximum score goes from 13 at 4.1 to 31 in the following sessions: these are probably different versions of the task and should not be compared without reviewing them.

Concurrent. Outside its domain, the highest relation is with: Phonological awareness (4.1, r = .26); Phonological awareness (4.2, r = .33); Phonological awareness (5.2, r = .30).

Against reading difficulty and comprehension. Against persistent difficulty, AUC .55–.68. Against poor comprehension one or two school years later (non-circular criterion), AUC .58.

Against an external criterion, years later. Against low Woodcock-Muñoz reading fluency in 2021/22 (chapter, in Spanish), AUC .58–.61 (2 time points; 117–121 children).

For publications

The LEEDUCA platform task “Pseudoword stress” (code 2154; LEEDUCA battery) assesses prosodic sensitivity (stress). Stimuli are presented auditorily and the child responds by selecting on the screen. The time limit is 90 seconds. It is administered individually on a computer at school. In the 2012 and 2013 cohorts it was administered in 3 sessions, from 4 to 5 years of age. The main outcome is the number of correct responses. Stability across successive administrations (median interval of 4 months) was ρ = .33. Stability across time points was ρ = .33. Against later poor reading comprehension, AUC = .58.
