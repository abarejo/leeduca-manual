Theoretical framework

Choosing, between two forms that sound the same, the correctly spelled word (for example, vaca vs. baca) measures orthographic knowledge: the representations of specific words in the mental lexicon. This is what allows a word to be recognized at a glance, without converting it letter by letter.

How it is formed. According to the self-teaching hypothesis, each time the child successfully decodes a new word, something of its written form is stored. Phonological decoding works as an “internal teacher” (Jorm and Share 1983). Computational models show that this mechanism is enough to learn thousands of words and that initial phonological deficits are enough to produce dyslexia (Ziegler, Perry, and Zorzi 2014). A systematic review of 62 experiments confirms the role of phonological recoding in all writing systems (Li and Wang 2023). It also works in Spanish, except with infrequent spellings of a sound, which need more exposure (Ferroni and Diuk 2014).

What the task measures. Orthographic choice captures both knowledge of specific words and more general orthographic knowledge. Choosing correctly does not depend only on being able to read the word (Compton et al. 2020). In Spanish, the orthographic lexicon contributes to reading competence beyond decoding, and it is refined with years of practice (Cuadro and Costa 2014).

What our data show. 2021 α = .80 and stability between terms ρ = .52, lower than for reading aloud. Even so, it is one of the tasks that best predict low reading fluency years later (median AUC .76, maximum .80). This is to be expected if it measures how many words the child has consolidated, which is precisely what fails in dyslexia in a transparent orthography.

Each form (platform code) has its own norms, because the forms need not measure on the same scale.

These are norms by time point (school year × term). For dates between assessments and time points without a normative sample, study M3 proposes continuous norms on months of instruction, not on age, with relative age within the school year as an adjustment.

Forms: can they be pooled?

Forms given at the same time point are compared. They are considered equivalent if the mean difference is smaller than 0.20 SD and the SD ratio is between 0.80 and 1.25. If the same children took them, linear equating is also computed, which puts the second form on the scale of the first, together with the correlation between forms (parallel-forms reliability). If the groups differ, the difference mixes form and cohort.

For publications

The LEEDUCA platform task “Orthographic decision” (codes 1937 and 2039; DIS-ESP battery) assesses orthographic knowledge. The examiner presents each item orally, supported by a picture on the screen, and records the child’s oral response. It comprises 26 items. The time limit is 120 seconds. It is administered individually on a computer at school. In the 2010 and 2011 cohorts it was administered in 5 sessions, from 6 to 8 years of age. The main outcome is the number of correct responses. In our data (2021 analyses), internal consistency was α = .80 (N = 3,922, 26 items). Stability across successive administrations (median interval of 8 months) was ρ = .63 (median of 3 session pairs; range .47–.66). Stability across time points was ρ = .43–.60. Against later poor reading comprehension, AUC = .55–.61.
