Theoretical framework

Letter knowledge is the ability to recognize each grapheme and associate it with its name or sound. It is the gateway to the alphabetic principle. The platform measures it in two ways: naming letters, and choosing the letter with which the name of a picture begins, which also requires isolating the first sound.

What the evidence says. Together with phonological awareness and rapid naming, it is one of the three major predictors of reading. Its weight is similar in English, Spanish and Czech (Caravolas et al. 2013). In Spanish, letter knowledge is the best predictor of simple syllable reading; phonological awareness weighs more for complex syllables (Martínez and Goikoetxea 2020). The full review of the evidence is in the letter knowledge pilot task sheet (in Spanish).

What our data show. Both tasks have very high internal consistency (α = .90 and .97). Letter naming is stable between terms (ρ = .73) and predicts low Woodcock-Muñoz fluency years later with a median AUC of .71. As a skill with a ceiling, it discriminates mainly at ages 4 and 5.

For publications

The LEEDUCA platform task “Initial letter identification” (codes 1884 and 4047; LEEDUCA battery) assesses alphabetic knowledge (phoneme–grapheme mapping). Each item is presented with a picture on the screen; the examiner gives the instruction orally and records the child’s response. It comprises 24 items. The time limit is 120 seconds. It is administered individually on a computer at school. In the 2010, 2011, 2013, 2014 and 2015 cohorts it was administered in 6 sessions, from 5 to 7 years of age. The main outcome is the number of correct responses. In our data (2021 analyses), internal consistency was α = .90 (N = 3,273, 24 items). Stability across successive administrations (median interval of 4 months) was ρ = .57. Stability across time points was ρ = .57. Against later poor reading comprehension, AUC = .58–.69.
