Theoretical framework

Reading syllables, words and pseudowords aloud measures written word recognition: converting letters into sounds and accessing the word. It is the decoding component of the simple view of reading (Hoover and Gough 1990) and the core of dyslexia according to the IDA definition (What we measure in light of the new definition of dyslexia).

What distinguishes each list. Dual-route models separate two procedures. The sublexical route converts graphemes into phonemes and is the only one that works for pseudowords. The lexical route recognizes the whole word, and it shows in words being read faster than pseudowords (lexicality effect) and frequent words faster than rare ones. In Spanish, children use both from Grade 2 (Avdyli, Castejón, and Cuetos 2014; Davies et al. 2013). That is why the platform has both lists: pseudowords measure pure decoding, and the difference with words estimates how much orthographic lexicon the child has built. Length (from one to four syllables) makes serial reading visible: the more the child depends on letter-by-letter conversion, the more each added syllable costs.

In a transparent orthography, what discriminates is speed. In Spanish, almost all children read accurately by the middle of Grade 1. Children with dyslexia are accurate but read more slowly, and their reading resembles that of younger children of the same reading level: it is delayed rather than deviant development (Davies, Cuetos, and Glez-Seijas 2007). A meta-analysis of 79 European studies concludes that reading speed distinguishes readers with dyslexia in all orthographies and ages, whereas accuracy depends on age and transparency (Carioti et al. 2021). In German, 70% of slow readers in Grade 1 were still slow in Year 8 (Landerl and Wimmer 2008). That is why the score in these task sheets is correct responses per minute, not correct responses.

What our data show. These are the most reliable and valid tasks on the platform:

  • Reliability. Stability between terms of ρ = .65 to .81, and 2021 α between .73 and .97.
  • External validity. They predict low Woodcock-Muñoz reading fluency years later with a median AUC of .72 to .77 (chapter, in Spanish). The longer lists (three- and four-syllable words) predict best.
  • Delay or deviance. Comparing pseudowords with words among children of the same reading level separates delay from deviance (Norms by reading level, in Spanish).

Floor effect. At 5.2 (form 72), 5.3 (form 72) and 6.1 (form 72) P10 coincides with the worst possible score (at 5.2 (form 72) and 5.3 (form 72), P25 too): many children are tied at the minimum and the norms do not distinguish between them. At those time points this task cannot be used to grade difficulty.

These are norms by time point (school year × term). For dates between assessments and time points without a normative sample, study M3 proposes continuous norms on months of instruction, not on age, with relative age within the school year as an adjustment.

Forms: can they be pooled?

Forms given at the same time point are compared. They are considered equivalent if the mean difference is smaller than 0.20 SD and the SD ratio is between 0.80 and 1.25. If the same children took them, linear equating is also computed, which puts the second form on the scale of the first, together with the correlation between forms (parallel-forms reliability). If the groups differ, the difference mixes form and cohort.

For publications

The LEEDUCA platform task “Pseudoword reading: two syllables” (codes 1934, 72 and 74; LEEDUCA and DIS-ESP battery) assesses decoding (sublexical route). The child reads aloud the stimuli shown on the screen and the examiner records each correct response or error. It comprises 12 items. The time limit is 30 seconds. It is administered individually on a computer at school. In the 2010, 2011, 2012 and 2013 cohorts it was administered in 19 sessions, from 5 to 8 years of age. The main outcome is the efficiency (correct responses per unit of time). In our data (2021 analyses), internal consistency was α = .96 (N = 1,156, 12 items). Stability across successive administrations (median interval of 4 months) was ρ = .74 (median of 15 session pairs; range .04–.88). Because the task is timed, α tends to overestimate reliability; stability across administrations is the preferred estimate. Stability across time points was ρ = .43–.84. Against later poor reading comprehension, AUC = .55–.65.
