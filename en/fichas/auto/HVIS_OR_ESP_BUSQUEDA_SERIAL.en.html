Serial visual search

Theoretical framework

Visual-attentional span is the number of visual elements processed simultaneously within a string. On the platform, a string of letters, numbers or symbols is briefly presented and the child chooses what they saw. Serial search asks whether there is an O among groups of Qs of different sizes. The sustained attention task (UNED battery) consists of 50 trials in which the child decides whether the name they hear matches the picture; despite its name, the catalog describes it as an index of inhibitory control. Reading requires processing several letters at once and maintaining attention along the line.

What the evidence says:

  • Visual-attentional span predicts reading beyond phonemic awareness. In 417 French children in Grades 1, 3 and 5, it predicted reading independently of phonemic awareness, especially for irregular words (Bosse and Valdois 2009). Orthographic depth modulates how this attention is distributed: advanced bilinguals reading in French and Basque distributed it more evenly than those reading in Spanish and Basque (Antzaka et al. 2018).
  • Visual search is associated with reading, with small samples. In 16 children with dyslexia and 16 controls, slow serial search was associated with visual-attentional span and with irregular word reading, not with phonology (Lallier, Donnadieu, and Valdois 2013). Visual scanning measured in preschool predicted learning to read in Italian children (Ferretti, Mazzotti, and Brizzolara 2008).
  • Sustained attention is related more to comprehension than to word reading. In 245 poor readers in Grades 4 and 5, it explained unique variance in comprehension, but not in reading accuracy or fluency (Macdonald et al. 2021).

The evidence is weaker than for phonological processes and there are almost no studies in Spanish.

What our data show.

  • Letter and symbol subtests. With 6–10 points and a single session, AUC from .47 to .61: separately they are not interpretable.
  • Numbers and letters (36 items, ages 4–5). α = .60 and AUC of .66–.67 against persistent difficulty and of .65 against later low comprehension. It is the best in the family, as expected of alphanumeric stimuli.
  • Numbers (16 items). α = .56 and almost zero stability across school years (ρ = .05–.19). AUC from .54 to .69.
  • Serial search. α = .74, ceiling of 11–20% and AUC from .58 to .63.
  • Sustained attention. Despite an α of .95, it shows no validity: extreme ceiling (median 45 out of 50), stability of ρ = .03–.16, null correlation with the other domains and AUC from .43 to .56. It should not be used until the data have been reviewed.

Reliability

There are no two time points with the same children (at least 80). α (2021) = .74.

Validity

Concurrent. Outside its domain, the highest relation is with: Phonological awareness (4.3, r = .31); Phonological awareness (5.2, r = .31); Phonological awareness (6.2, r = .21).

Against reading difficulty and comprehension. Against persistent difficulty, AUC .58–.63. Against poor comprehension one or two school years later (non-circular criterion), AUC .56–.65.

Against an external criterion, years later. Against low Woodcock-Muñoz reading fluency in 2021/22 (chapter, in Spanish), AUC .53–.67 (3 time points; 118–271 children).

For publications

The LEEDUCA platform task “Serial visual search” (code 1987; LEEDUCA battery) assesses spatial orienting and visual search. Each item is presented with a picture on the screen; the examiner gives the instruction orally and records the child’s response. It comprises 24 items. The time limit is 120 seconds. It is administered individually on a computer at school. In the 2010, 2011 and 2013 cohorts it was administered in 3 sessions, from 4 to 6 years of age. The main outcome is the number of correct responses. In our data (2021 analyses), internal consistency was α = .74 (N = 1,266, 24 items). Against later poor reading comprehension, AUC = .56–.65.
