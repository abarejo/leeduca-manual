Inhibition

Automatically generated task sheet · F_EJEC_INH_INHIBICION

Automatically generated task sheet (E · Decision with response time; quality class A). Generated on November 27, 2026 with scripts/R/ficha_generica_v3.R --tarea F_EJEC_INH_INHIBICION. Figures come from the data; the theoretical framework, interpretation and team review are missing. Spanish name: Inhibición. Spanish version.

In one sentence. Inhibition, from age 4 to 7 (3,738 administrations in 4 cohorts). Stability across time points ρ = .05. AUC .39–.54 against persistent reading difficulty.

Description

Codes 1863
Battery UNED
Format rapid naming of a series
Items — (1863) according to the platform
Task family E · Decision with response time
Score Correct responses
Instructions (platform) A group of numbers is shown on the screen. In the center there is a number in black that could be one, two, three or four times repeated. On each side of this number there is a number in grey. One of the gray numbers corresponds with the number of times the central (black) number is repeated. The child clicks the mouse button (right or left) situated on the same side that the number pointing the times the central number is repeated. In the example above, the right mouse button should be clicked.

Validity

Concurrent. Outside its domain, the highest relation is with: Phonological awareness (4.2, r = .21); Letter knowledge (4.3, r = .00); Letter knowledge (5.2, r = .04); Rapid naming (RAN) (5.3, r = -.07); Word and text reading (6.2, r = .02); Reading comprehension (7.3, r = -.07).

Against reading difficulty and comprehension. Against persistent difficulty, AUC .39–.54. Against poor comprehension one or two school years later (non-circular criterion), AUC .46–.51.

Against an external criterion, years later. Against low Woodcock-Muñoz reading fluency in 2021/22 (chapter, in Spanish), AUC .41–.63 (5 time points; 117–256 children).

For publications

The LEEDUCA platform task “Inhibition” (code 1863; UNED battery) assesses inhibitory control. The child names a series of familiar stimuli on the screen as quickly as possible, and the examiner records correct responses and errors. It comprises 121 items. The time limit is 300 seconds. It is administered individually on a computer at school. In the 2010, 2011, 2012 and 2013 cohorts it was administered in 6 sessions, from 4 to 7 years of age. The main outcome is the number of correct responses. In our data (2021 analyses), internal consistency was α = .98 (N = 2,501, 121 items). Stability across time points was ρ = .05. Against later poor reading comprehension, AUC = .46–.51.
