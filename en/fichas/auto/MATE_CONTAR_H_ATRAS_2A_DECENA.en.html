Theoretical framework

Counting forwards and backwards, and reading Arabic numerals, measure symbolic number knowledge: the verbal sequence of numerals and its correspondence with digits. This is learned knowledge, distinct from the approximate number sense (comparing quantities), and it forms the basis of school arithmetic.

What the evidence says:

  • Counting in preschool predicts mathematics in the first school years. Mastery of counting principles at age 5 predicts the risk of mathematical difficulties in Grade 1 (Stock, Desoete, and Roeyers 2009). In Spanish children, the verbal number sequence (procedural counting) was the preschool task with the most weight in Grade 2 mathematics achievement (Mercader Ruiz, Presentación Herrero, and Siegenthaler Hierro 2017).
  • Knowing Arabic numerals is one of the two pillars of early arithmetic. In 569 children, only number knowledge and quantity discrimination uniquely predicted arithmetic progress in the first school year (Malone, Burgoyne, and Hulme 2020). Children with mathematical difficulties already show symbolic deficits in preschool, and those deficits persist (Desoete et al. 2012).
  • Counting backwards is more demanding than counting forwards. It requires retrieving the sequence in reverse order and holding it in working memory, which is why it discriminates better at age 5.

What our data show. The four tasks have high internal consistency (α = .84–.95), but they were given in one or two sessions and there is no stability between terms. They predict later reading fluency poorly (AUC .53–.59), which supports their discriminant validity. Against low arithmetic at ages 10–11, counting backwards in the second ten is the most specific: AUC .67 vs. .51 for reading (Numerical cognition, in Spanish).

Reliability

There are no two time points with the same children (at least 80). α (2021) = .93.

Validity

Concurrent. Outside its domain, the highest relation is with: Phonological awareness (5.3, r = .46).

Against reading difficulty and comprehension. Against persistent difficulty, AUC .63. Against poor comprehension one or two school years later (non-circular criterion), AUC .60.

Against an external criterion, years later. Against low Woodcock-Muñoz reading fluency in 2021/22 (chapter, in Spanish), AUC .56 (1 time point; 327–327 children). Against low arithmetic at ages 10–11 (provisional criterion, in Spanish), AUC .67.

For publications

The LEEDUCA platform task “Counting backwards (11–20)” (code 2032; LEEDUCA battery) assesses verbal number sequence. The child names a series of familiar stimuli on the screen as quickly as possible, and the examiner records correct responses and errors. It comprises 6 items. The time limit is 30 seconds. It is administered individually on a computer at school. In the 2011 and 2012 cohorts it was administered in 2 sessions, from 5 years of age. The main outcome is the number of correct responses. In our data (2021 analyses), internal consistency was α = .93 (N = 1,227, 6 items). Against later poor reading comprehension, AUC = .60.
